Changing cell size restarts the scene. APIC through the grid keeps sloshing waves about three times longer than Müller's FLIP with particles moving by their own velocity, and is closer on the dam break and run-up too. FLIP share applies only to FLIP: 1 keeps every particle's own velocity, 0 takes the grid's, which damps. Gauss-Seidel is Müller's original; at 1 cm cells its 50 passes leave still water moving. CFL 2 halves the steps in fast scenes; it lowers the run-up from 2.20 to 2.03 H (2.35 measured) and leaves the dam break as close.
